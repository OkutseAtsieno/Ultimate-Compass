Key to the terms

What the words and columns on this page mean.

HSR (high-speed running)
Distance a player covered while moving faster than the high-speed threshold. It shows how much hard running a player did, not just how far he travelled.
Sprint (m)
The part of the running that was at sprint speed, faster than high-speed running.
Distance (m)
Total ground covered while the player was tracked.
Top speed (km/h)
The fastest smoothed speed. It is trusted only when the player was tracked cleanly for at least 10 s; otherwise it is faded and marked with *.
Clean tracking (s)
Seconds of unbroken tracking, with no gaps filled in. The longer it is, the more a player's speed numbers can be trusted.
Lower bound
A number that can only be too small, never too big. A player is counted only while he is tracked, so real distances are at least this much.
Tracking ID
The label for one continuously tracked player (such as W05 or L06). If tracking broke, one player can appear under two IDs.
Ball control (s)
Time a player was the nearest to a slow-moving ball while his team had it. Goalkeepers count.
Pass candidate
The ball moved from one player's control to a teammate's. It is a candidate, not a confirmed pass, because the video is analysed automatically.
Contested ball (duel)
Players from both teams were close to the ball at the same time, so neither side clearly had it.
Turnover
The ball ran loose and then the other team gained control of it.
Carry
The same player kept or regained control and moved on with the ball.
Confidence: high, medium, low
How sure the pipeline is about an event. High means both players held the ball for a clear spell; low means the evidence was thin, so check it on video.
Tracked player-step
One player at one moment of the clip. One step is 0.12 s, so the 30-second clip has 250 steps. Heat maps count these.
Heat map
The pitch cut into 5 m squares. Brighter squares are where a team spent more of its tracked time.
Position accuracy
About 1 m: how close a dot on the pitch is to where the player really stood.
